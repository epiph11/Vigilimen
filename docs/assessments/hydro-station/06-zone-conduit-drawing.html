02

What this drawing argues

Three claims a reviewer should be able to take up.

Note 1 · Partitioning

Purdue describes function, not consequence

Z-05a outranks Z-04 despite sitting a level higher, because the engineering workstation holds the toolchain that can reprogram Z-01 and Z-02, and the backup store decides whether a bad day is a bad week or a bad year. A partition drawn by redrawing the network diagram produces zones that match the switches you already have — which is a description of the past.

Note 2 · Security levels

Nothing is rated SL-T 4

SL 4 implies extended resources directed at this asset. A 20 MW station that is not systemically significant does not obviously attract that. The contrary argument — that TRITON demonstrated nation-state interest in safety systems — is real and is set out in document 03 §5. The position taken here is that an SL-T you cannot fund is worse than an honest SL-T 3, because it produces a paper requirement rather than a control.

Note 3 · Boundary

The SUC boundary creates one known gap

The portfolio control centre is out of scope and has no assessment of its own, yet C-08 grants it supervisory authority over the unit. That is an unassessed trusted path, drawn deliberately rather than quietly omitted. Closing it is an asset owner decision recorded in document 01 §8.

The constraint that overrides every control on this drawing

No security control may be placed in the trip path of Z-01. A control that can fail closed on a protective function has made the plant less safe, not more. Every treatment for Z-01 acts on its management interfaces, on access, or on detection — never on the protective action itself. This is the direct lesson of TRITON and it is carried verbatim into the Cybersecurity Requirements Specification.
