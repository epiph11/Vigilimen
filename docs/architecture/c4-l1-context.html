What the crossed arrow costs

The machine-learning plane advises; it does not act

A predictive maintenance model at S19 raises a work order, and a person decides. No closed-loop optimisation, no remote setpoint adjustment from the enterprise plane. That is a weaker demonstration and a better architecture, and it is written into ADR-005 as an accepted capability cost rather than discovered later as a limitation.

Why the plant is external

The plant is not ours to change

Drawing the OT environment as an external system rather than a component of Vigilimen is the first architectural statement the diagram makes. The plant existed before the software and will outlive it; the system integrates with it on the plant's terms. A context diagram that swallows the plant produces a programme that assumes it may modify controllers.

Why the vendors are on the drawing

The highest-consequence path is a supplier

Vendor remote access is out of scope as a system and firmly in scope as a risk — in the worked hydro assessment it is the highest-consequence conduit in the plant, because its consequence is the maximum of everything it can reach. Leaving it off the context diagram is how it ends up ungoverned.

Why the analyst is a user

Security is an operating role, not a phase

The OT security analyst appears beside the operator and the planner because the assurance work does not end at handover. They own the SL-A gap — the delta between the security level each zone targets and the level it actually achieves — and that delta is the remediation backlog. It is the loop the whole assurance programme exists to run.
